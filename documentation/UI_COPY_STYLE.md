# UI Copy Capitalization

Use [APA title case](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/capitalization/title-case)
for screen and sheet titles, section headings, field labels, tabs, menu items,
buttons, status badges, feedback titles, and Help Center question headings.

Intentional uppercase text is a design exception. Preserve the existing uppercase
headings, section labels, badges, and their styling. Do not remove uppercase
styling or rewrite explicitly uppercase text to enforce title case.

- Capitalize the first word and the first word after a colon, em dash, or sentence-ending punctuation.
- Capitalize nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, pronouns, and words with four or more letters.
- Keep short articles, conjunctions, and prepositions lowercase within a title: “a,” “the,” “and,” “as,” “for,” “in,” “of,” “on,” “to,” and “by.”
- Capitalize adverb particles in actions such as “Sign Up,” “Log In,” “Sign Out,” and “Turn Off Flashlight.”
- Capitalize major words on both sides of a hyphen, such as “One-Time” and “Multi-Currency.”

Examples: “Pay in Full,” “Pay Remaining,” “Payment History,” “Note (Optional),”
“Proof of Payment,” “Confirmed by You,” and “Login With Google.”

Use sentence case for descriptions, instructions, validation messages,
placeholders, notification bodies, and explanatory alerts. When a sentence
refers to a particular control, match the control’s label: “Choose Pay in Full.”
Inline payment sentences such as “John pays You” remain sentences.

Preserve brand spelling, acronyms, currency codes, email addresses, and all
user-entered names and content. The lowercase “ambagan” wordmark is intentional.
Do not apply a runtime title-case formatter to user data. Write each static
display label in its intended case, independently of internal IDs, saved values,
and date formats. Retain intentional uppercase styling as part of the design.
